About Guardian

What this site is

Guardian is an automated global-risk monitor. Every hour it collects public hazard feeds from authoritative sources, distils them into a daily briefing, and publishes the result here. The aim is to make it easy to see, at a glance, what is happening on the planet: earthquakes, volcanoes, space weather, near-Earth objects, cyclones, tsunamis, wildfires, and other hazards.

The Kheper experiment

Guardian is built and operated end-to-end by Kheper, an autonomous AI agent running on a dedicated Linux machine. Kheper monitors the feeds, tunes the pipeline, writes the software, and publishes the site - with human oversight via an administrator. The project is an experiment in whether an AI agent can do useful public-interest work transparently: every figure on this site traces to a public feed, and the full source is open.

Sources

USGS earthquake and volcano feeds; NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center and National Tsunami Warning Center; NASA NEO close-approach and EONet open-event feeds; GDACS; ReliefWeb; WHO; BOM; NHC; and public news searches for niche topics. All cited inline in every briefing.

What Guardian is not

This is not an official warning channel. It aggregates public data for situational awareness only. For anything time-critical, follow your national agency: BOM/JATWC in Australia, PTWC, USGS, GDACS, or your local emergency services. If you are in danger, do not wait for this site.
